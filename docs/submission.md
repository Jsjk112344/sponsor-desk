## 500-word description

Sponsor Desk addresses an event-organizer workflow: finding plausible sponsor
candidates, deciding which deserve attention, and reviewing the first exploratory
pitch. Evidence, selection, and draft review often become disconnected conversations.
This project brings those steps into a Switch room where two existing coding agents
and a human organizer can see the same brief, corrections, and decision context.

Sourcer begins by restating the effective event brief and waiting for human
confirmation. It then gathers evidence of sponsorship listings. Every
candidate carries an event edition, sponsorship tier, source URL, and observation
timestamp. A prior roster listing is not treated as evidence of payment, current
budget, or buying intent. Fit remains a labeled inference, and missing information
remains unknown. An unavailable service is never evidence against a prospective sponsor.

The human selects candidates through ordinary chat. Shortlist permits preparation
of an unsent draft. Not_now defers work, and skip excludes a candidate from the next
step. These choices do not authorize delivery. Sourcer hands the selected candidate
to Outreach by its full participant name, in the original thread, with a versioned
contract containing the confirmed brief version and shareable receipts.

Outreach uses that handoff to prepare a role-addressed exploratory pitch. It does
not invent a person, email address, product claim, or sponsor relationship.
Where authorized access exists, the intended persistence path is Source’s versioned
outreach draft save and reader. Internal Argus Sales targets organizer accounts;
Sponsor Desk does not manufacture an organizer identity for a sponsor brand just
to satisfy that API. Private Source sessions and licensed evidence do not become
shareable simply because two agents belong to the same room.

Human review applies to the exact draft version in its original thread.
Approval never transfers to a different recipient, revised brief, or later draft.
The room retains immutable Task and Decision documents with the human message
reference and body hash. Current Switch documentation marks native Tasks as not
ready, so this entry discloses its document fallback instead of claiming unsupported
functionality. Review changes no backend product-claim approval and never grants
sending authority. A request to “just send it” must produce a Room Rule 1 refusal.

Reproducibility starts with public fixtures. Five company listings were checked
against CNCF’s KubeCon India 2025 transparency report. The event brief is fictional,
and the candidate analysis and pitch are authored demonstration outputs. The local
contract rehearsal runs without model credentials, Docker, paid research, or email
infrastructure. Tests exercise identity, thread, version, retry, correction, exclusion,
and never-send boundaries. CI publishes the observed rehearsal transcript.

Switch is intended to supply shared context, addressed delivery, trusted participant
identity, and durable room records. A standalone local rehearsal cannot prove those
properties, so Console import, room execution, authenticated Argus access, and the
video remain explicit acceptance checks. The public repository contains
original integration materials, not proprietary Ducket implementation or customer
data. Development began after the published competition deadline; this draft makes
no claim of timely entry, completed live integration, measured commercial outcomes,
or manual submission. The owner must verify remaining checks and handle submission.
